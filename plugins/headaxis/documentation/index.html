User guide

Ullhexa HeadAxis Head-controlled 3D audio spatialization by Ull Hexa. Audio Direction Think of the head as the listener. Sound arrives from your position looking at the display; you hear it through the head's ears at the angle shown. Seeing the back means sound arrives from behind the listener. Seeing the top means it arrives from above. Controls - X rotates the head horizontally. Y rotates it vertically. Both wrap continuously; 180 degrees on each is straight ahead. - Drag inside the head display to rotate it. Hold Shift for finer adjustment, including during a drag. Double-click the display to return X and Y to the forward position. - Z sets distance from 20 cm to 50 m. Scroll up inside the head display to move closer; scroll down to move farther away. The displayed head size follows distance. - Drag a knob or scroll over it to change its value. Hold Shift for finer control. - Gain is the final output level, from -12 to +12 dB. Double-click the fader for 0 dB. Click its title to lock or unlock mouse adjustment; locking does not bypass the current gain. Input Modes Mono combines the input into one source and produces a spatial stereo output. Stereo preserves an existing stereo field while applying direction and distance cues. It does not identify or independently move objects embedded in a recording. Dual treats the left and right inputs as separate sources, so turning the head rotates their relative positions. Shared content in the inputs can behave differently from fully independent sources. Playback and Floor Click the Ull Hexa logo to open settings inside the head display. Choose Speakers or Headphones to match how you listen. Headphones retain interaural timing cues; Speakers adapts the processing for loudspeaker listening. Legacy retains the original, more strongly colored model. Select No floor, Wood, Tile, or Concrete. Floor reflections add material-dependent coloration and a sense of distance. Reduce playback level before comparing extreme gain or distance settings. How It Works HeadAxis combines frequency-dependent head shadow, ear-shaped spectral cues, ear-to-ear timing where appropriate, distance attenuation and air filtering. Optional reflections add a floor cue. Direction can remain apparent even when the overall levels in the two channels are similar. This is a procedural spatial effect, not a personalized measured HRTF or an exact reconstruction of a recorded scene. Results depend on the source and listening setup. Interface and Recall Settings offers six wallpapers and 75%, 100%, 125%, or 150% scaling. Set current UI as Default stores wallpaper and scale for new instances. Existing instances retain their own settings in the DAW project. Close settings by clicking the logo again or outside the head display. Audio parameters can be automated by your host. X and Y have a numeric wrap point; host interpolation across that boundary can differ from continuous mouse movement. Common front-facing positions are centered at 180 degrees to avoid that boundary.
